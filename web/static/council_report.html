1. Executive Summary & Purpose

This briefing packet provides the empirical justification, machine-learning microclimate evidence, and formal statutory resolution for the allocation of $10,000,000 in municipal capital funds to establish four (4) permanent climate resilience cooling hubs and one (1) rapid-deployment mobile hydration tanker in Dallas.

Urban heat island (UHI) effects create severe, life-threatening microclimate temperature spikes across our city. Machine-learning inference via Google Cloud BigQuery ML (heat_offset_model) trained on ground-level NOAA Heat Watch observational campaign telemetry demonstrates that thermal exposure is concentrated heavily in neighborhoods characterized by high poverty, low tree canopy, older residential structures lacking central HVAC, and high transit dependency.

OFFICIAL PROCEEDINGS OF THE CITY COUNCIL

CITY COUNCIL RESOLUTION NO. 2026-HEAT-01

An Ordinance Authorizing the Expenditure of Capital Improvement Funds for Emergency Extreme Heat Resilience Hubs, Prescribing Operational Standards, and Mandating Title VI Civil Rights Compliance.

WHEREAS, extreme heat represents an acute and escalating public health threat, with the National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration (NOAA) recording recurring summer temperatures exceeding 100°F and intra-urban heat island anomalies spanning up to 9.2°F across municipal neighborhoods; and

WHEREAS, empirical machine-learning predictive modeling utilizing Google Cloud BigQuery ML demonstrates that heat vulnerability is disproportionately concentrated in census tracts characterized by high rates of poverty, transit dependency, and uninsulated pre-1975 housing stock; and

WHEREAS, an independent algorithmic Title VI Equity Audit has confirmed that allocating resilience funding to the designated candidate facilities eliminates disparate impact and prioritizes historically underserved and medically vulnerable residents;

NOW, THEREFORE, BE IT RESOLVED BY THE CITY COUNCIL OF THE CITY OF DALLAS:

SECTION 1. CAPITAL APPROPRIATION. The sum of $10,000,000 is hereby authorized and appropriated from the Municipal Capital Resilience Fund for the procurement, construction, and modernization of extreme heat life-safety infrastructure.

SECTION 2. FACILITY SITING AUTHORIZATION. The City Manager is authorized to execute modernization agreements for the four (4) permanent resilience hubs and one (1) mobile hydration unit designated in Exhibit A (Geographic Siting Roster), adhering strictly to 1,200-meter pedestrian walkability standards.

SECTION 3. OPERATIONAL READINESS STANDARDS. All authorized facilities shall maintain backup solar microgrid or generator capacity capable of sustaining continuous commercial air conditioning and clean air filtration for not less than 72 consecutive hours during grid power interruptions.

SECTION 4. TITLE VI CERTIFICATION. The Office of Equity and Inclusion shall conduct ongoing quarterly audits to ensure that cooling center operating hours, language interpretation, and security protocols strictly adhere to Title VI of the Civil Rights Act of 1964 (42 U.S.C. § 2000d).

SECTION 5. EFFECTIVE DATE. This Resolution shall take effect immediately upon its passage and approval by the City Council.
